Fertility

Learn about fertility, ovulation, menstrual cycles, factors that may affect fertility and when to seek professional advice.

What is Fertility?

Fertility refers to the ability to become pregnant. It can be influenced by age, ovulation, reproductive health, lifestyle and many other factors.

Understanding Ovulation

Ovulation is the release of an egg from an ovary. It usually occurs once during a menstrual cycle, although the timing can vary between individuals and cycles.

Fertile Window

The fertile window is the time around ovulation when pregnancy is more likely to occur. Sperm can survive in the reproductive tract for several days, while an egg remains available for fertilization for a limited time.

Factors That May Affect Fertility

  • Age
  • Irregular ovulation
  • PCOS and other hormonal conditions
  • Endometriosis and other reproductive health conditions
  • Certain medications or medical treatments
  • Smoking and other lifestyle factors
  • Some health conditions affecting either partner

Supporting Reproductive Health

  • Eat a balanced and nutritious diet.
  • Maintain regular physical activity.
  • Get adequate sleep.
  • Avoid smoking and recreational drugs.
  • Manage ongoing health conditions with professional guidance.
  • Track menstrual cycles when useful.

When to Seek Fertility Advice

If pregnancy has not occurred after a period of regular attempts, or if there are concerns such as very irregular periods, known reproductive conditions or previous fertility problems, speaking with a healthcare professional can be helpful.

Important: Fertility can involve many factors and may affect either partner. A healthcare professional can provide individual assessment and appropriate guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can irregular periods affect fertility?

They can. Irregular periods may sometimes indicate irregular ovulation, which can make it harder to predict fertile times.

Does age affect fertility?

Yes. Fertility can change with age, and the effect varies between individuals.

Can PCOS affect fertility?

PCOS can affect ovulation and may make conception more difficult for some people. Many people with PCOS can still become pregnant with appropriate care.

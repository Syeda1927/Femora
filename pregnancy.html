Pregnancy Care

Learn about pregnancy stages, common symptoms, healthy habits, warning signs and when to seek medical care.

Understanding Pregnancy

Pregnancy is a period of physical and emotional changes as a baby develops inside the uterus. Every pregnancy can be different, and regular prenatal care is important.

Common Early Pregnancy Symptoms

  • Missed period
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Breast tenderness
  • Fatigue
  • Increased urination
  • Changes in appetite

First Trimester

The first trimester covers the early weeks of pregnancy. Major developmental changes take place during this time, and many people experience tiredness, nausea or breast tenderness.

Second Trimester

During the second trimester, some early pregnancy symptoms may become less noticeable. The abdomen usually begins to grow as the pregnancy progresses.

Third Trimester

The third trimester is the final stage of pregnancy. The baby continues to grow and the body prepares for childbirth.

Healthy Pregnancy Habits

  • Attend regular prenatal appointments.
  • Eat a balanced and nutritious diet.
  • Drink enough fluids.
  • Get adequate rest and sleep.
  • Follow advice from your healthcare professional.

Medicines During Pregnancy

Not every medicine is suitable during pregnancy. Always discuss medicines, supplements and herbal products with a qualified healthcare professional before using them.

When to seek urgent medical care: Severe abdominal pain, heavy vaginal bleeding, fainting, difficulty breathing, severe headache, vision changes or other serious symptoms should receive prompt medical attention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is nausea common during pregnancy?

Yes. Nausea is common, especially during early pregnancy, although its severity can vary.

Why are prenatal checkups important?

Regular prenatal care helps monitor the health of the pregnant person and the developing baby and allows potential concerns to be identified early.

Can every pregnancy feel the same?

No. Pregnancy symptoms and experiences can vary significantly between different people and even between pregnancies in the same person.

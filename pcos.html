PCOS & PCOD

Learn about PCOS and PCOD, their symptoms, possible causes, lifestyle factors and when to seek medical advice.

What is PCOS?

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a common hormonal condition that can affect menstrual cycles, ovulation and other aspects of reproductive health.

What is PCOD?

PCOD is a term often used to describe hormonal and ovulation-related changes involving the ovaries. The terms PCOS and PCOD are sometimes used interchangeably, although they are not always medically identical.

Common Symptoms

  • Irregular periods
  • Changes in menstrual flow
  • Acne or oily skin
  • Excess facial or body hair
  • Hair thinning or hair loss
  • Weight changes
  • Difficulty with ovulation

Possible Causes and Risk Factors

The exact cause of PCOS is not fully understood. Genetics, hormonal factors and insulin-related changes may all play a role.

Lifestyle and Self-Care

  • Eat a balanced and varied diet.
  • Stay physically active according to your ability.
  • Maintain a regular sleep schedule.
  • Manage stress through healthy activities.
  • Track menstrual cycles and symptoms.

How is PCOS Evaluated?

A healthcare professional may consider symptoms, menstrual history, physical examination and appropriate laboratory tests or imaging. There is no single test that confirms PCOS in every person.

When to seek medical advice: If you have persistent irregular periods, unusual bleeding, severe symptoms, concerns about fertility or other ongoing changes, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can PCOS affect periods?

Yes. PCOS can be associated with irregular or less frequent periods because ovulation may not occur regularly.

Can someone with PCOS become pregnant?

Yes. Many people with PCOS can become pregnant. However, irregular ovulation may sometimes make conception more difficult, and medical guidance can be helpful.

Is PCOS the same for everyone?

No. Symptoms and their severity can vary from person to person.

WOMEN'S HEALTH

Menopause

Understand menopause, perimenopause, common symptoms, lifestyle support and when to talk to a healthcare professional.

What is Menopause?

Menopause is a natural stage of life when menstrual periods permanently stop because the ovaries produce much less reproductive hormones. Menopause is confirmed after 12 consecutive months without a menstrual period, when there is no other obvious cause.

What is Perimenopause?

Perimenopause is the transition period leading up to menopause. During this time, hormone levels can fluctuate and menstrual cycles may become irregular.

Common Menopause Symptoms

  • Hot flashes
  • Night sweats
  • Irregular periods
  • Sleep difficulties
  • Mood changes
  • Vaginal dryness
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Fatigue

Hot Flashes

Hot flashes are sudden feelings of warmth, often involving the face, neck or chest. They may last for a short time and can sometimes be followed by sweating.

Bone Health

Changes in estrogen levels around menopause can affect bone health. Regular physical activity, adequate calcium and vitamin D, and appropriate medical advice can help support healthy bones.

Heart Health

Heart health remains important throughout and after menopause. A balanced diet, physical activity, healthy weight when appropriate, avoiding smoking and regular health checks can support cardiovascular health.

Menopause Self-Care Tips

  • Maintain a balanced and nutritious diet.
  • Stay physically active.
  • Keep a regular sleep routine.
  • Stay hydrated.
  • Avoid smoking.
  • Use relaxation techniques to manage stress.
  • Discuss bothersome symptoms with a healthcare professional.

Treatment and Medical Support

Menopause symptoms can sometimes be managed through lifestyle changes, non-hormonal treatments or hormone therapy, depending on individual circumstances. Treatment decisions should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

When to seek medical advice: Talk to a healthcare professional about unusual bleeding after menopause, severe symptoms, persistent pain or any symptom that significantly affects your daily life.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age does menopause usually happen?

Menopause most commonly occurs between the ages of 45 and 55, although timing varies from person to person.

Can menopause affect sleep?

Yes. Hormonal changes, night sweats, hot flashes and mood changes may contribute to sleep difficulties.

Is bleeding after menopause normal?

Bleeding after menopause should be evaluated by a healthcare professional, even if it happens only once.
